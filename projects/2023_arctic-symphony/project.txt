Arctic Symphony is a four-fold exploration integrating architecture, performance, natural processes and photography. Born from my graduation project at TU Delft, the work investigates how built space interacts with time, decay and the rhythms of the Arctic landscape.

Inspired by the subtle changes of a hidden pond near my childhood home overlooking the Oslo fjord, the project treats time as a fluid force — something architecture can embrace rather than resist. The design delves into themes of decay and regeneration, culminating in a live performance that unified architecture, dance and music.

The photographic component documents the landscape at different seasons and times of day, tracing the same spatial experience across months. These images informed the material palette and tectonic choices of the architectural proposal, closing the loop between observation, research and making.
