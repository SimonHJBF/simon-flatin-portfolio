Assembling the Arctic Symphony was the inaugural performance that first brought together the central themes of what would later become my graduation project. Staged at TU Delft, it explored how architecture might be assembled, performed and experienced rather than simply built and inhabited.

The work drew on fieldwork in Svalbard and the polar coastline, translating landscape observations into a live sequence of spatial events — sound, movement and physical material combined into a single durational act. The temporal dimension of the Arctic, where seasons compress and expand time dramatically, was made tangible through the choreography of the performance itself.

This early assembly was a laboratory: testing how architecture, dance and sound could coexist without hierarchy, and what a building might communicate when experienced as a performance score rather than a static object.
