The Bjørnåsen House Addition is a residential extension project in the forested hillside district of Bjørnåsen, south of Oslo. The existing cabin-style house sits on a steep site with long views toward the fjord, and the addition was designed to expand the living area while preserving the intimate, embedded quality of the original structure.

The design takes its cue from the delta shelter typology — a simple triangulated form that follows the slope of the land and minimises its footprint. Clad in dark-stained timber, the addition reads as an extension of the forest floor, its angular silhouette echoing the pitch of the surrounding treetops.

Inside, the new volume houses a generous open-plan living and dining area that steps down with the terrain, bringing the forest floor visually into the heart of the house. A generous glazed gable opens directly to the trees, while a sheltered south-facing terrace captures the long afternoon light.
