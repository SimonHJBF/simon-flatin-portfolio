Sometimes the most instructive building is a small one. The chicken coop — designed and built by hand in the garden of a house on the outskirts of Oslo — became an exercise in material honesty, structural economy and the pleasure of making something complete.

The structure uses rough-sawn Norwegian spruce, left to weather naturally, jointed with traditional mortise-and-tenon connections wherever the chickens' wellbeing and a few wood screws would allow. The roof pitches in two directions to shed snow, and a south-facing run gives the birds light in the few hours of Oslo winter sun.

Beyond its obvious function, the project was a reminder that architecture's principles apply at every scale: proportion, orientation, material, light and the relationship between inside and outside are just as alive in a 4m² hen house as they are in a public building.
